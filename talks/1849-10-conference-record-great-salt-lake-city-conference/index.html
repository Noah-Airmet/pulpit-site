[Leaf 11] 6th Oct. 1849. 10 a.m.

Minutes of the General Conference held in the Bowery in G.S.L. City - pursuant to vote at last April conference ¶

Present. B. Young. H.C. Kimball - D. Spencer - J. Smith. W. Richards - Jas.[?] - L. Snow - W.W. Phelps. J.M. Grant. F.D. Richards. E. Snow - P. Pratt. J. Taylor ¶

Called to order by Pres[t]. Spencer - Choir sung "On the mountains tops appearing" Prayer by H.C. Kimball Choir sung a hymn - ¶

H.C. Kimball. it appears the S[ain]ts. are not turned out this morning, & are not enough to transact the bus. of the Conf. as there is much to be done, & it is beneficial to all - we are all of same family, one our God & one Savior - & therefore the bus. interests every man. this ought to be thot. of a little more than what it is - this cause that we are espoused. I laid hold of to continue in it thro' life & to all Eternities. this world is only my second Estate - & if I keep it. it is glory, & everlasting to all Eternities. read the works of Abram[?] on these matters - we want the [people] to feel as we feel - for the welfare of this [people] & the inhabitants of the Earth - shall we debar one portion of this community from what we av. obtained - how shd. we feel if they had rec'd. it. & wod. not commit it to us - we want the [people] to take an int[erest] with us in bearing off the K[ingdom] to all the nations of the Earth - the time is come when some of the 12 & 70's will go - how do you feel on the subject ye El. of Is[rael] - you ought to prepare your hearts & be ready to go forth - mormonism will stand & every man & woman that recs. it. will live for ever - this old house will tumble to pieces & will build a better one - & if our bodies fail it shews we shall av. a better one - evil savour & wretchedness shall fall on those who oppose this work - & good will rest on this [people] - dont prophecy anything that you are not willing to carry out - we av. commenced a machine so large. that we can pick up a nation at once - God established this work by J.S. & shall we become like drones in the house of Is[rael]. I say no - let us arise & make our election sure - this [people] are going to be rich & it will overthrow many - but a few years & you will all be rich. ¶

Pres[t]. Young thinks it not best to continue meeting long this forenoon - but give the men an opportunity to finish the doors & windows - & then be ready to come this P.M. - as soon as the Council House is built there will be meetings there every day & a reformation will take place in Is[rael]. your minds will be led to that, instead of carousing & dancing - when I hear preaching & music my mind is full of glory, joy peace & happiness - but my body is clogged with about 190 lbs weight of tabernacle ¶

John Taylor thinks there is no need of dismissing - if [we] are too largely[?] come we need not debar ourselves of our privileges & blessings we are met to confer tog[ether]. about the things pertaining to the K. of God - & we may av. to attend to the int. of some 100s. who are not here - we av. gone thro' many sceneries & endured many things - what will try one man will not try anot[her]. some are tried with poverty & ot[hers]. with riches - there is a great deal of diff. in temperaments - it is necessary we shd. go thro' every kind of trial that we may experience & understand them - who do we want to pilot men from the States - some one who has been the road - the S[avior]. sent forth to redeem the Earth - he was buffeted & cuffed around. & to make himself a sacrifice - for he was tried same as us - he knows what we are capable of enduring, & what we are worth - Jesus was an hungered 40 days - he was tempted of the Devil - forsaken by his friends - he understands all the sit[uation]s. - he cried out my God why hast thou forsaken me - av. you felt the powers of darkness bear over you, so as he - av. you seen the glory of the world - so as he - there is nothing we have thro' but he has - av. we not all had to grapple with poverty & sickness - if we are to go to heaven like the Sectarian Priest, we shd. no nothing - the S[avior]. av. put us into a School where we can learn & live for ever - we shall never die - we av. the seed of immortality in us - & we shall live for ever - there is something in our religion different to any one else - our Cov[enan]ts. are all Eternal they av. hold of Eternity - our bodies may slumber & our Sp[irit]s. go into anot[her]. existence - we go to possess a glory that has been revealed to us - we are a nucleus [?] pertaining to our Chil[dren] & Chil. Chil. worlds without end - if we are got to be K. & P. we are to be rulers - & if they dont no how to rule they will be in a poor place - they will be same as Napoleon at Elba - he an Emperor & no one to rule over - there are many great privileges placed on us - & if we dont make a ri[ght] use of them we shall want a Samaritan to wash the mud of us - we talk of the great missions to the nations - but we are all some objections to it - but if men dont go - they may go to the devil if they are a mind to - remember there are K. & Priests now sitting on thrones who wod. be glad to exchange situations with you - & go & preach the gospel - I feel by & by the El. of Is. will come but in & I want to go on missions - many hate to ask for a mission - but want to go - but Pres. Young I once say if you want 50 men to go they are on & to go - if you place some men in adversity you cant crush them - they are like [a] jack in the box - you cant put them down - always are heads up - whatever comes - you who are got bags of gold feed the hungry & clothe the naked & God will bless you - dont get your heart strings buckled up - but what you sow that shall you reap. ¶

B.Y. wanted those [who] expect to return with the mail this coming week - we want them to give in their names & their means of conveyance - ¶

The Bishops ought to av. a door keeper to each door - fix the seats for the P.M. - I realize we ought to bear off the K. to the nations of the Earth - the [people] in general, are a good [people] - I shall seek to do this [people] good & my heart is like a chariot of Amminadab[?] - & will in my mind - I feel happy for this ordinary shelter - I shall seek to do this [people] good & my desire[?] Sal[vation]. to the H. of Is. & want to see Zion built up - ¶

Choir sung "Come let us anew" Benediction by C.C. Rich dismissed for one hour - ¶

[Leaf 1] Oct. 6. 1849 1 1/2 P.M.

Conference again convened purs[uan]t. to adj[ournmen]t. - Called to order by D. Spencer ¶

Present H.C.K. - W.R. - J. Smith - L. Snow - F.D. Richards, E. Snow - J.M. Grant - B. Young. O. Pratt. C.C. Rich. Choir sung a hymn - Prayer by John Young - ¶

H.C. Kimball. there is lying before us much bus. to transact - & many little think they are to do a deal of labor - so it is thot. best to proceed to bus. the 1st. item will be to raise funds to bring the poor to this place - most of you are aware of the cov[enan]t. made in Nauvoo - to help all to come to this Valley - I av. seen Pres[t]. Smith so filled with the H.G. when he anticipated coming into this Valley - we are here, rich to what we are here & are a healthy country - when the 12 started for Engl[an]d and I saw & many Pres. so faint he cod. not stand on his feet - & dying on the right & left - & here we are healthy & plenty to eat, drink & to wear - & I prophesy you shall never see less while you live - some of us came here 2 yrs ago - & we ret[urned]. there - & came again with our families & 1000s. - & we are now more rich & wealthy than we ever were before - we are made a Cov[enan]t. - shall we fulfil it or shall we not. if you do, signify it by saying amen - (loud shouts) now if you are 10. but it in a few[?] months you shall have four fold - & the d[evil] has [n]o power in your tithes & your offerings & I will pour out a blessing that it shall overflow - now let every man & woman take hold. dont send your agent with less than 1,000 & then you will cause a day of rejoicing among the poor in Ill[inois] - the S[pirit]. will pour riches into this place as fast as you want it - this is but a drop to a mighty shower ¶

B.Y. I move we raise a fund to bring the poor to this place - sec. Carried - H.C.K. we want all to vote whether they belong to the Ch. or not - to gather up the poor - sec. Carr[ied]. B.Y. I move there be a com[mittee] of 5 to raise funds to send back to gather up the poor - sec. Carr[ied]. I nominate Willard Snow to be one of that com. - sec. & Card. John S. Fullmer ————— Carr. Lorenzo Snow ————— Card. John D. Lee ————— Carrd. F.D. Richards ————— Carr. I move that E. Hunter carry the funds back - buy cattle ^& av. the oversight of the whole & bring the poor to this place - sec. Card. B.Y. [struck: Amasa Lyman] C.C. Rich gather up funds in California, as agents, for this ^Perpetual fund - sec. Carr. J. Taylor moved that the whole of this concern be under the direction of the First Pres[idenc]y. - sec. Card. ¶

B.Y. I move that C.C. Rich go on a mission to Western California & assist Amasa Lyman to preach & gather up the S[ain]ts. sec. Card. Addison Pratt ⎫ James Brown ⎬ go to the Society Islands & preach the Gospel - sec. Carr. Hiram Blackwell ⎭ ¶

B.Y. motioned that one of the 12 go to Italy & anot. to Denmark & Germany - viz Lorenzo Snow & Joseph Toronto are nominated to go to Italy - sec. carried motioned that Erastus Snow & Peter Hansen go to Denmark - sec. Carried. ¶

H.C.K. cond. bro Hansen has translated the B. of M. into his native [struck] language - he came by a dream to this land & obtain[ed] a copy of the book - translated - & is now ready to return - & a better boy never lived - & Jos[eph]. Toronto also - they will preach the Gospel to the [struck: nations] - they will heal the sick & cause the old to come to life - he is filled with dreams & visions & prophesyings - & I never cared for persecution. the hotter it is the more glorious it is ¶

B.Y. I move that Joseph W. Johnson, & Joseph W. Young take a mission to England - sec. Carr. John Taylor take a mission to France - sec. Carried H.C.K. to Taylor - we want to try your words of this morning (laf) moved & sec'd. that Curtis E. Bolton take a mission to France with El. Taylor - Carr. moved & sec'd. that Fras[?] an. Pomeroy take a mission to the Coast with El. Rich - Carrd. ¶

B.Y. rose - concerning the 12. when they take a mission to any nation - they dictate the affairs there - same as I do here - the enquiry may be made can L. Snow dictate any where but in Italy - yes they dictate in all the world - two of them may Council tog[ether]. & send El. where they please & as they deem wisdom - as we app[ointed]. L. Snow. & E. Snow - are they any right any where else - yes I wish they wod. open the door all over the world - if any of them wish to take any from this place. they will council with the First Pres[idenc]y. - if they see any one professing to be an El. in the Ch. & bring disgrace on his P[riesthood]. he has right to walk up & take his licence & send him about his business - but it may be well to call the brandts[?] tog. & they will cut them off - as the [w]rite[?] is[?] they are always willing to part with - ¶

H.C.K. there are numerous bus. to come before this Conference - one is for the welfare peace & happiness of this community so as to benefit themselves - you are aware masons & mechanics of all sorts are left their bus. to attend to farming - now we av. grain enough to last over anot. year - we want you all to go into your former trades. classify yourselves & come into some order & manufacture the thing in this place instead of buying all from the States - & we are also to consider the Settlement in Sandwich[?] Valley - with Jos. Horsley[?]. C. Shumway & [struck: 30 others] ^Seth Taft - we except those going who are not timber & not easy handled. we wish to keep them in this mill until they become more reliable ¶

B.Y. I move that F.D. Richards goes to England to preach the Gospel — sec. Carr. —"— Job Smith go with him to England ————— sec. Carr. —"— Haden W. Church[?] go to England ————— sec. carr. H.C.K. all those El. who have been appointed to go on missions are expected to start this week - ¶

B.Y. I move that Isaac Morley, Chs. Shumway & Seth Taft be the men to take the Presidency ^of the Settlement in of the Sandpitch Valley - Carr. J.B.[?] read the letter from Smith & Benson at Independence Rock - ¶

H.C.K. spoke of the classifying the tra[des] - some to lumbering - others to handling wood &c & some to tanning. the nations will soon be in motion - & we shall av. the Potters making as fine chinas as any in Staffordshire - some to Paper mill - making oil - shall we sit down in idleness - no. let us make the things ourselves - let us begin this moment. classify ourselves & go into manufacturing - we are now sending out thousands of dollars for things that we can make ourselves - we want a glass maker to make our window glass, tumblers - &c - ¶

motioned & seconded that H.G. Sherwood build a Glass Factory as soon as circs. will admit - Carried - H.G.S. I no you cant make it to advantage - this year - you cod. not get it into operation under 2 yrs - there are no tools or implements but what can be made in this Valley - ¶

H.C.K. we are workmen in Silk Cotton & Woollen - we can do this ourselves ^& not be dependent on the nations - I hope & pray that every man may be inspired to carry on these things - & bring down the prices of things - for the Stores av. shaved us & galled us all the days of our lives. let us come down & dont take soul & body at the first rake - for that man who does it, will not prosper - & if he dont atone in this world he will in the next - for his sins will come against him in the next world - this [people] never were beat for goodness by any o[ther] [people] - take counsel then & be subject to the powers that be - as a general thing I never saw a family of children more obedient to their parents than this [people] are to their leaders - ¶

P.P. Pratt. your experience in this Valley has taut you many things - for advantage & disadvantage - such as Shoemaker going into lumbering - a clothy maker[?] who used to be a cheese maker - an unacquainted man going into the Kanyons - no man can no many trades - it is not required of him. let every man wherein he is called - abide - through necessity we are all been farmers whether we would or not. &c &c ¶

H.C.K. It is motioned & sec. that George B. Wallace & John S. Higbee take a mission to England - Carr. John Pack go to France with Elder Taylor ————— sec. Carr. - ¶

John Taylor stated that it is proposed to av. a Carrying Co. from the States to this place - upon national principles - if it cod not been for the El. going to the nations to preach - we shd. av. been always poor - we are now beginning to wake up. how smart we are to pay .50 a pound for leather & throwing thousands of hides away - if it had not been for these Gold mines we shd. av. been in poverty - labor is wealth. the carrying business is going to be a lucrative business. ¶

I move that a Company be organized to carry goods & merchandize from Mo. River to this place - sec. Carried B.Y. I propose they start a Passenger Co. from this place to the States forthwith - J.T. thats what I go in for. that will pay better than the other - P. Pratt that is as easily done as I cod. make the road thro the Kanyon. I began with only provisions for one week - & can get the road thro' by Monday night next. - C.C. Rich - any persons who are desirous to go with me to the West - will let me no early - so as to make arrang[emen]ts. ¶

motion for adjournment until tomorrow at 10 a.m. at this place - The Official members notified to meet at Early Candlelight - ¶

Choir sung a hymn Benediction by Isaac Morley ¶

[Leaf 2, cont.] Early Candlelight Oct. 6. 49

Conference of officers Present B.Y. - H.C.K. - W.R. - L. Snow. F.D. Richards. J.[M.] G[rant]. on stand "Glorious things of thee are spoken" - was sung Prayer by J.M. Grant "From Greenlands Icy mountains" - was sung ¶

B.Y. is not sufficiently well to raise my voice (& called the bre[thre]n nearer to the stand) my text is "Joseph the Seer was called to bring forth the Gospel of Salva[tio]n. to the human family & to build up the K. of God on the Earth to the downfall of Satan's reign" - the foundation is laid & it is for us the El. of Is. to build thereon - my heart is like the Chariot of Amminadab - I labor to the extent of my strength & ability - I am full of joy & comfort - & every man who does the will of the Far. in heaven is full of joy - & can rejoice with those that rejoice - & wod. as soon go to the house of mourning - I feel that of. better to preach a funeral sermon than to go to house of dancing - when I see all creation - the world & all its inhabitants bound to be ruled by an all wise Providence & it will result to the glorification of the S[ain]ts. - for the S[ain]ts. will reign over them - they cant do any thing agst. the truth - every time the devil has kicked the L.D.S. they are kicked them into better circs. - we av. the best house we ever av. for a winter - in Nauvoo we were 7 years - but av. no place to meet in in winter - the Lord wont let us think of every thing at once - there is not a person who is going but is sorry to leave us - & yet glad to go - every man that has the Sp. of God in him wants every living thing to hear his voice - the very design of our Far. in heaven was that our bodies wod. go into the Earth - I wod. not want to live in this world so very long - this old body has shook with the ague for 30 yrs - I want this old body to be renewed every voice to be as that at the end of the world wod. hear me - or be as quick as light to be in their midst & roar among them ¶

[Leaf 3] p. 4 — (B.Y. continues, Early Candlelight Oct. 6)

I av. confidence in a good man as much as in God according to his capacity - I am as true to my bre[thre]n as the sun is to rise upon us - if I dont love the bren it is bec. I dont no [k]now. the El. of Is. are more obedient than a family of chil[dren]. - they quarrel so do chil. but are they not quiet when they come home - yes - I hate to see the bren go away I love them - we are now in a sit[uation]. to begin to av. the comforts of life - we anticipate better times - last Spring it was all hurrah for the Corn & Wheat - hurra[h] for the time will soon come that the El. will av. to go to the nations - you see a good crop - good Corn - beans - melons - the L[or]d. has blest us - we live & are under shelter & we mean to line it up & whitewash it - & if I'm able I will stand here & thunder to the El. of Is. post[?] - I say the foundation is laid - av. you got this building built - we av. got to erect that building - no man is fit to handle Gold until he is past lusting after it - no man is fit. to av. farms wives &c - until he does not lust after it - when the El. of Israel can ride on the wings of the Al[mighty]. that little strip of 40 miles wide & 500 long is not the only stripe of Gold - I expect the L[or]d. will close up the vail by & by - and when the El. of Is. can command it - they will av. it - when the El. of Is. can handle governmental rule - they will control it - & say do this, or go there - we can build up the fabric by going to France Germany Italy - China or Gold mines - all the time in every place - & we can operate to our own destruc[tion]. every where in every thing that we do, in all of our movements - when men can act on rite[ous] principles the world is under their feet with all its pomp & splendor, for their whole aim is to build up the K. of God on the Earth - if their object is to build up the K. of God - & their only object - that is their place - & tho a man shd. go to the Gold mine & come back to make himself happy he is not where he shd. be - unless his sole object is to build up the K. of God - we av. a good place to meet in - thank God & the bren - when I came in here on 20 Sept. last year - I saw in the fort every day from 20 to 150 men & boys wrestling &c nothing to do - & it was contention & hurra' - I saw nature acted out there - how like the devil they ride - get out of the way - but I av. not be annoyed that way this Summer - there is not a loafer in this town that I no of - I av. laid plans & done the best I cod. - & we av. raised the grain - & done first rate - we got up this shade by having the dinner on 24 July & it is to be praised - we are now in a sit. to say to Lumbermen & Sawyers go into a Kanyon - & no man shall av. the [k]nack[?] of getting one log & shove it into market - if our lumbermen wod. work on safe prin[ciples]. we cod. av. it cheaper if I shod. see a man steal a saw log of mine I shod. be tempted to shoot him dead - it is abhorent to me a cursed dam'd thief - thieves will be in our midst so long as we av. the Goats & Sheep tog[ether]. - I sometimes feel like Jesus when he took a scourge of ropes & cried get out of my way ye old thieves - ¶

bren you av. seen my course for years back - I used to feel that every El. in the Church knew more than I did - it was astonishing when the S[avior]. called me to be an Apostle - if I do the best I can & he dont add to that. its his look out not mine - my mind wod. be torn to pieces if it were not for Calving[?]. there is not an El. of Is. but will off with his coat & labor all the days of his life - you young men had better go while you are young - just as fast we make improvements here. we will bring men into their own order - I wod. rat[her]. furnish wood at 400 a cord - than bring it here at 8. & run my chance - we want men to go to handling in the Falls[?] - every man can have work - every man to his bus. & let men work to their form[er]. & trades - I say to Blacksmiths - they rule the price your course is impolitic - if you av. confidence in me & this community - let the general matters be dictated by them - wod. I put price on a Blacksmith, Shoemaker & masons work - no - & its impolitic to continue tog. - dont throw fetters on your feet - you will be hated for ever if you put on the grip[e]s on any man - there is many a man that I've seen working on the Temple - bare feet - & av. nothing to eat but dry bread - it is Gods truth the poor av. built the Temple - & the rich av. done nothing - now I am sworn to feed the poor I'm determined they shall fare well - I mean to monopolize all for who work on the public buildings - I dont want to see men poor - I am the boy than can tell them what to do with their money - we want a paper mill - cotton factory - oil mill - next year I want to walk over that rim of the basin - & drop down - to raise Cotton & Cane - I want men with means to be dictated - & I wod. rejoice to av. men go & buy what we want - the Sheep & machinery - why av. I implicit confidence in men - because they av. implicit confidence in me & I av. confidence in myself - if men will only invest their own Capital that is all I ask - I dont no what the L[or]d. is going to appoint to me - I calculate we shall av. a tabernacle that will cost as much as the Nauvoo Temple did - 3 or 4 Baptismal fonts - & then the Temple - but what we shall accomplish I dont no - if we get up the Camping[?] &c - I do no that thousands of dollars will center here - & if we dont do it - it will center in other places - I wod. like to see the Pres[iden]ts. of 70. begin to meet here - & the H.P. - & Bishops - night after night. may the God bless you - altho' I av. not s[aid]. a quarter I intended to - ¶

9 P.M. ¶

I want every man that is going East to be armed to the teeth - as no men ever stood in need of arms as this Season - according to request of the [people] last fall - I put out a paper currency - then they wanted to av. the Gold in their fingers & I av. got what must that does first rate - & if any man can get as much pure Gold out of 2 Eagles & 1/2 as out of the 20. piece. I will give him the 20. piece — & called on the bren to bring in their dust to be coined - ¶

"All is well" was sung. Dismissed by G.B. Wallace 1/4 past 9. ¶

B.Y. called the Cong[regatio]n. to be composed while we transact the bus. of the day - the 1st. will be to present the diff[eren]t. Present -- the quorum of the First Presidency, several of the quorum of the Twelve Apostles, the Patriarch, the Presidency of the Stake, the Presidency of the High Priests' Quorum, the High Council, and Thomas Bullock, clerk of conference, together with a large number of high priests, seventies, elders, &c. ¶

President H. C. Kimball then made some excellent remarks upon the importance of the Saints going to work with their might, to fulfil the covenant made in the temple of the Lord in Nauvoo, that they would not cease their exertions until the poor were gathered to this safe and quiet resting place; and the importance of the elders bearing forth the gospel to the nations, that the honest in heart in all the world might have the opportunity of participating with us in the blessings, light, and liberty of the gospel. ¶

Elder John Taylor followed with some excellent remarks on the same subject. ¶

President Brigham Young arose and gave some instruction about finishing the Bowery, and said, "I feel happy for this shelter from the rays of the sun and the blasts of winter. I feel well in my mind; my heart is like the chariot of Ahinadab, full of joy. I realize we ought to bear off the kingdom to the nations of the earth. This people as a whole are a good people, and I shall seek to do them good, and bring forth salvation to the whole house of Israel, for I want to see Zion built up." The conference adjourned for one hour. ¶

In the afternoon a great deal of important business was done. A vote was taken to raise a fund to gather the poor to the Valley. A committee was appointed to collect the fund by subscriptions. It was resolved that the "Perpetual Emigration Fund" be under the control of the Presidency. The appointments of C. C. Rich to Lower California, Elders A. Pratt, James Brown, and Hyrum H. Blackwell, to the South Sea Islands, and the missions to England and various European countries, were unanimously sanctioned. It was motioned and carried that every man, as far as possible, should establish his own branch of business in the Valley; and, as a beginning, H. G. Sherwood was requested to establish a glass manufactory as soon as possible. The Great Salt Lake Carrying Company was organized. Conference adjourned till next day. ¶

Sunday, October 7th, conference met pursuant to adjournment. It was unanimously voted to sustain the quorum of the First Presidency, the quorum of the Twelve, John Smith as Patriarch of the whole church, Willard Richards as church historian, Orson Hyde as president of the quorum of the Twelve Apostles, John Young, Reynolds Cahoon, and George B. Wallace as the presidency of the High Priests' Quorum, Joseph Young, Levi Hancock, Henry Herriman, Zera Pulsipher, A. P. Rockwood, B. L. Clapp, and J. M. Grant as the first presidency of all the quorums of the Seventies. Also to sustain the High Council, the presidency of that stake, and, finally, all the constituted authorities of the church in all the world. Several new places of settlement were appointed, the account of which is contained in the "General Epistle." Conference adjourned for an hour. ¶

In the afternoon the conference assembled and opened as usual, when the several bishops proceeded to administer the sacrament. ¶

Elder Parley P. Pratt related his experience at the commencement of the work, and the time when the prophet, Joseph Smith, stood up in the midst of the congregation, and by revelation pointed out each individual to be ordained to the Melchisedec priesthood; and also related a miraculous case of healing a woman who was near her death. He then delivered a discourse on the subject of tithing: said he, the principle of tithing is not peculiar to this church or people -- it is as old as the Bible. We see it illustrated in Abram's day, for when he held the power of the kingdom of God, Abram paid one-tenth of all he possessed. Where is there a Latter-day Saint who does not know and understand that it is by the power of the Melchisedec priesthood that we receive forgiveness, and justification, and eternal life. To fulfil the law of tithing, a man should make out and lay before the bishop a schedule of all his property, and pay him the one-tenth of it; when he has tithed his principle once, he has no occasion to tithe again, but the next year he must pay the one-tenth of his increase, and the tenth of his time, of his cattle, money, goods, and trade; and whatever use we put it to, it is still our own, for the Lord does not carry it away with him to heaven. We have to establish the government of God on earth, and that requires means, and a knowledge how to use it, and to have every thing written on "Holiness to the Lord." Let God have what is His, and every thing be under the control of the Lord, and not Caesar, or the world. If the people do not hold themselves responsible to help to carry off the kingdom of God, how do you expect we can do it ourselves. The Twelve and the elders are all minute men, and that is what makes us different to the rest of the world. ¶

Elders Erastus Snow and John Taylor spoke on the same subject. Elder P. P. Pratt then moved that this conference adjourn to the 6th of April next, at 10 o'clock, a.m., to meet in this place: carried. The choir then sung the doxology, and were dismissed with benediction by Willard Snow. President Brigham Young then blessed the people in the name of the Lord, when they separated rejoicing in the Lord for His many mercies, that He has extended to His Saints, and for the happy and glorious conference that they had been privileged in attending. ¶

In the evening the brethren who had been appointed to go on missions were called together for the purpose of receiving instructions, &c., whereupon the first Presidency proceeded to lay their hands on the quorum of the Twelve, and set them apart for their respective missions. ¶

The Twelve then laid their hands on the elders who had been appointed missions to the different nations of the earth, and set them apart, predicting their success, and the remarkable scenes that will transpire during their absence from their families. A joyful meeting was continued until twenty minutes past ten, when the meeting was dismissed, all enjoying the peaceable influence of the Spirit of God, and filled with faith that the Lord God of Israel will speedily work a great and glorious work on the earth. ¶

Collation notes

  • The manuscript now transcribed is the original, not the copy. The Bullock fair copy (8 leaves) previously cited as this file's preferred witness carries its own note, "See following for original minutes with more detail," pointing to the 11-leaf item transcribed above. That pointer has now been followed. The copy is retained as a secondary witness and the source of the attendee list section.
  • Leaf order. The delivery API returns the leaves out of document order. The reconstruction above rests on three independent signals that agree: the session headings themselves (Oct 6 10 a.m. → Oct 6 1½ p.m. → Oct 6 candlelight → Oct 7 10 a.m. → Oct 7 2 p.m. → Oct 7 evening); Bullock's ink leaf-numbering, visible as "4" on leaves 3 and 7, "5" and a circled "9" on leaf 8, and "10)" on leaf 9; and sentence-level continuity across the breaks, most clearly leaf 11's "dismissed for one hour" running into leaf 1's "Conference again convened purs. to adj." Leaf 10 is the folded wrapper, bearing Bullock's docket and the bleed-through of leaf 9.
  • The Perpetual Emigrating Fund committee is corrected. The prior heavyweight pass, reading the fair copy, named the committee "Willard Snow, John [?] Hunter, Erastus Snow." The original minutes record five separate nominations, each moved and carried in turn: Willard Snow, John S. Fullmer, Lorenzo Snow, John D. Lee, and F. D. Richards. Edward Hunter was a distinct motion — to "carry the funds back - buy cattle ^& av. the oversight of the whole." The word "Perpetual" is an interlineation, inserted by caret into the motion making C. C. Rich an agent to gather funds in California; the fund is not called "Perpetual" in the earlier motions on the same leaf.
  • "O. Pratt" vs "P. P. Pratt." Bullock's initial before "Pratt" at the tithing discourse (Oct 7 afternoon) is formed as a round letter that reads more naturally as "O" than "P," and he writes "O.P. Pratt" at the adjournment motion. The Millennial Star assigns both the Kirtland reminiscence and the tithing discourse to Parley P. Pratt. The manuscript itself settles the question against a literal "Orson": in the evening setting-apart meeting, F. D. Richards is blessed to "be a help meet to thy serv. Orson in G[reat]. B[ritain]. & Wales" — Orson Pratt was presiding over the British Mission and was not in the Valley. The reading is therefore left as [O./P.] Pratt in place rather than silently resolved, and the discourse should be attributed to Parley P. Pratt.
  • The docket says "Sandwich Islands"; the minutes say "Society Islands." Bullock's wrapper docket summarises the missions as being to "Europe, California, Sandwich Islands, &c.," but the motion in the body sends Addison Pratt, James Brown and Hiram Blackwell "to the Society Islands." The body is the contemporaneous record and the Society Islands is correct; the docket conflates the two Pacific groups, as period usage often did.
  • "Sandpitch Valley." Bullock consistently spells the Sanpete/Sanpitch settlement "Sandpitch." Left as written. This is the Isaac Morley company's settlement, later Manti; the minutes place its presidency (Isaac Morley, Charles Shumway, Seth Taft) and record Morley notifying his company to be ready "two weeks tomorrow."
  • The Millennial Star summary independently corroborates the manuscript on the covenant-to-gather-the-poor remarks, the "chariot of Amminadab" (the Star prints "Ahinadab"), the Sherwood glass manufactory vote, the Carrying Company, the PEF being placed under the direction of the First Presidency, the sustaining list, the adjournment to April 6, and the evening setting-apart meeting closing at twenty minutes past ten. Where the two differ, the Star compresses; it does not contradict.
  • What the Star omits entirely and the manuscript preserves: the whole October 6 candlelight officers' meeting, including Brigham Young's discourse on classifying the trades and his call for the brethren to bring in their gold dust to be coined; the individual missionary blessings; the votes laying out cities at Captain Brown's neighborhood, Utah Valley and Sandpitch Valley; John Forsgren's call to Sweden; the ox-team pledges to assist the incoming companies from Weber River; and Bullock's note that after Peter Hansen's blessing the speaker "then spoke in tongues on his head" and "all the Congregation jumped to their feet, like Electricity."
  • This conference adjourned to "the 6 April next," matching the companion 1850-04-conference-record-great-salt-lake-city-conference.md file (April 6, 1850) in this vault.
  • The same Millennial Star issue also printed a "General Epistle of the First Presidency" (dated Great Salt Lake City, Oct. 12, 1849) and a separate letter to Orson Hyde about the Perpetual Emigrating Fund. Both are distinct documents from this conference and are not included here.
